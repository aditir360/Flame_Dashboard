Oxygen Runway turns NASA’s BASS-II fire experiments from the International Space Station into something you can explore, question and take away.

What this is

An interactive view of 129 real tests from NASA’s Physical Sciences Informatics database (PSI-25), where solid fuels burned in microgravity. Filter them, compare them, ask questions of the data, and build a personal briefing.

Why we made it

Fire is truly among the most dangerous events on a spacecraft, and flames behave very differently without gravity. The Moon base is expected to hold 30% oxygen or more, which makes materials far easier to ignite. We wanted anyone to see what is known, and what isn’t.

Data: NASA Physical Sciences Informatics, investigation PSI-25, BASS-II experimental table (129 tests, aboard the International Space Station). Every figure is read directly from that table. Fuel groups are ours, based on the sample description. The table has no burn-out, blowoff or never-ignited outcome, so none is shown, except where its air-display column mentions extinction or blowoff. Not an official NASA product.
